Getting fabric care right makes a significant difference to how your garments look and how long they last. Here's a practical guide to caring for the fabrics we carry at KodTex.

## Cotton Fabrics

Cotton is the most forgiving fabric to care for. It handles machine washing well and is generally low-maintenance.

**Washing:** Machine wash on a warm (40°C) cycle for most cottons. For lighter colours, cool water (30°C) helps preserve the dye. For heavy duty cottons (denim, canvas), hot water (60°C) is fine and helps sanitise.

**Drying:** Cotton can be tumble dried on medium heat. Remove while slightly damp to make ironing easier. For heavyweight cottons, line drying prevents shrinkage.

**Ironing:** Iron on high heat. Cotton can handle it. Iron while slightly damp for best results — or use steam.

**Shrinkage note:** Cotton will shrink slightly in its first wash. Pre-wash fabric before cutting and stitching to account for this.

## Linen Fabrics

Linen is hardier than it looks, but needs a little consideration.

**Washing:** Machine wash on a gentle or delicate cycle with cool or warm water (maximum 40°C). Hot water can cause excessive shrinkage.

**Drying:** Line drying is preferred. If tumble drying, use low heat only and remove while still slightly damp. Never over-dry linen — it becomes brittle when bone dry.

**Ironing:** Iron on high heat while the fabric is still slightly damp. Linen irons much more easily when it has some moisture. Use steam. Iron on the reverse for a less polished look, or on the face for a crisp finish.

**The wrinkle reality:** Linen wrinkles. This is natural and part of its character. Some wrinkles will fall out naturally when worn or hung. For a pressed look, iron before wearing.

## Corduroy Fabrics

Corduroy needs a little extra care to protect its distinctive wale texture.

**Washing:** Always turn corduroy garments inside out before washing. This protects the surface texture from rubbing against other items. Machine wash on a gentle cycle in cool water (30°C max).

**Drying:** Tumble dry on low heat or line dry. Shake the garment gently when removing from the machine to restore the wales. Never dry on high heat — it flattens and damages the texture.

**Ironing:** Iron corduroy on the reverse side only. Use a pressing cloth if ironing the face side. Iron while slightly damp. Never press hard on the front — this crushes the wales permanently.

**Storage:** Hang corduroy garments where possible rather than folding. Long-term folding can create permanent crease lines in the wales.

## Twill Fabrics

Twill is typically robust and easy to care for.

**Washing:** Machine wash on a standard cycle at 40°C. For dyed twills, turn inside out to preserve colour. Wash dark twills separately for the first few washes.

**Drying:** Tumble dry on medium heat, or line dry. Twill holds its shape well.

**Ironing:** Iron on medium to high heat. The diagonal weave structure makes twill relatively wrinkle-resistant compared to plain weave fabrics.

## Shirting Fabrics

Shirting fabrics are designed to be washed and worn regularly.

**Washing:** Machine wash at 40°C. For white shirting, a hot wash occasionally keeps whites bright. For coloured shirting, cool water (30°C) preserves colour.

**Drying:** Hang to dry rather than tumble drying where possible. If tumble drying, remove while slightly damp.

**Ironing:** Iron on high heat with steam while slightly damp. Start with collars and cuffs, then work to the body. A proper ironing technique makes a huge difference to how a shirt sits.

## Suiting Fabrics

Suiting fabrics — especially blends — often require more careful handling.

**General rule:** Check the care label on any suiting fabric. Poly-viscose blends are often more robust than pure wool and can be hand-washed on gentle cycles. Pure wool suiting should be dry cleaned.

**Spot cleaning:** For minor marks, spot clean with a damp cloth and gentle detergent before reaching for the full wash.

**Storage:** Hang suits on proper suit hangers. Allow garments to air out after wearing. Use a garment bag for long-term storage.

## General Fabric Care Tips

1. **Always pre-wash fabric** before cutting and stitching — this accounts for any shrinkage
2. **Test for colourfastness** on a small piece before washing a full garment, especially for deep or bright colours
3. **Wash like colours together** — especially for the first few washes
4. **Don't overload the machine** — fabrics need space to move freely in water
5. **Use a gentle detergent** — harsh detergents degrade fabric fibres faster
