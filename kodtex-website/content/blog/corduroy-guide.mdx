Corduroy is one of fabric's most misunderstood materials. Many people associate it with old-fashioned school uniforms or the 1970s. That's a shame — because a well-chosen corduroy is one of the most versatile, tactile, and beautifully textured fabrics you can work with.

## What is corduroy?

Corduroy is a woven fabric with parallel raised ribs — called "wales" — running along its length. These wales are created by cutting loops of extra yarn, which creates the characteristic raised ridges and the distinctive soft texture.

The word "corduroy" likely comes from the French "corde du roi" — cloth of the king — which gives you a sense of its historical prestige.

## Understanding wale counts

The most important number in corduroy is the **wale count** — the number of ridges per inch. This single number determines how the fabric looks, feels, and behaves.

### Wide-wale (4–8 wale)
- Bold, pronounced ridges
- Heavy and structured
- Best for: outerwear, furnishing, upholstery, statement jackets
- Casual and fashion-forward

### Standard-wale (10–14 wale)
- The classic corduroy look
- Versatile weight and drape
- Best for: trousers, blazers, casual jackets, bags
- Works across casual and smart-casual

### Fine-wale (16–21 wale)
- Almost velvety texture
- Lightweight and elegant
- Best for: shirts, children's wear, lightweight tailoring
- Can look formal or casual depending on styling

## Corduroy compositions

Most corduroy is cotton. Here's what the main compositions mean:

**100% Cotton** — The original and best. Breathable, soft, washable, and gets better with age. Our most popular corduroy option.

**Cotton-Lycra Blend** — Usually 97% cotton + 3% Lycra. Adds stretch and shape recovery, making it ideal for slim-fit trousers and leggings. All the texture, added comfort.

**Cotton-Polyester Blend** — More wrinkle-resistant and durable. Often used in workwear and furnishing applications.

## Special finishes

Beyond wale count and composition, corduroy can have different finishes that change its character significantly:

**Piece-dyed** — The most common. Fabric is woven first, then dyed. Gives consistent, uniform colour.

**Yarn-dyed** — Yarn is dyed before weaving. This creates slight tonal variation in the wales, giving the fabric more depth and a handcrafted feel. Particularly beautiful in corduroy.

**Indigo wash** — Inspired by denim finishing. Deep indigo tones with natural variation. Highly popular with fashion labels.

**Structured corduroy** — A firmer hand, better suited to structured garments and furnishing.

**Dobby weave** — Small woven patterns incorporated into the corduroy structure. Adds visual interest beyond the basic wale.

## How to care for corduroy

Corduroy requires a little care to stay looking its best:

1. **Turn garments inside out** before washing to protect the wales
2. **Wash in cold water** on a gentle cycle
3. **Never use high heat** — it can flatten the wales
4. **Tumble dry on low** or line dry
5. **Iron on the reverse** while slightly damp, using a pressing cloth
6. **Store properly** — hanging is better than folding for corduroy garments

## Choosing the right corduroy

Use this simple guide:

| Application | Recommended Wale |
|---|---|
| Formal trousers | 14–16 wale |
| Casual trousers | 8–14 wale |
| Blazers / jackets | 8–14 wale |
| Heavy outerwear | 4–8 wale |
| Shirts | 18–21 wale |
| Bags and accessories | 8–14 wale |
| Sofas and upholstery | 4–8 wale (heavy duty) |
| Cushions and curtains | 8–14 wale |
