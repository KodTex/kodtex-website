Linen is one of the oldest textiles in human history. Made from the fibres of the flax plant, it has been worn in ancient Egypt, throughout the Roman Empire, and in every corner of the world for thousands of years. And for good reason.

## What makes linen different?

Linen has a unique set of properties that no other fabric quite replicates:

**It breathes like nothing else.** Linen is hollow-core — each fibre has a natural channel that allows air to flow through. This makes it exceptionally breathable and cool in warm weather, which is why it's the traditional fabric of hot climates.

**It gets better with age.** Most fabrics wear out over time. Linen does the opposite — it softens and improves with every wash, becoming more comfortable the more you use it. A well-made linen shirt can last a decade or more.

**It has natural texture.** The characteristic "slub" of linen — slight irregular thickening in the yarn — gives it a visual texture that's impossible to fake. Every length of linen looks slightly different, which is part of its appeal.

**It regulates temperature.** Linen keeps you cool in summer but has natural insulating properties that make it comfortable in cooler months too.

## Understanding linen weight

Like most fabrics, linen comes in different weights, each suited to different applications:

**Lightweight (100–140 GSM)** — Shirts, summer dresses, scarves, inner layers. Drapes beautifully and feels almost sheer.

**Medium weight (150–200 GSM)** — The most versatile. Shirts, trousers, kurtas, light jackets. Our best-selling shirting linen falls here.

**Heavy weight (200–280 GSM+)** — Suiting, structured blazers, outerwear, furnishing. Holds its shape and has substantial body.

## Pure linen vs linen blends

### 100% Pure Linen
- Maximum breathability
- Most pronounced texture and slub
- Most wrinkle-prone (this is a feature, not a bug — it's part of the natural character)
- Highest quality and longevity
- Needs a little more care in washing

### Linen-Cotton Blend (e.g., 55% Linen, 45% Cotton)
- Softer hand than pure linen
- Less wrinkle-prone
- Easier to care for
- A good entry point if you're new to linen
- Slightly less breathable than pure linen

Both have their place. Pure linen is for those who want the full linen experience — the texture, the breathability, the character. Linen-cotton is for those who want linen's aesthetic with a little more practicality.

## The wrinkle question

Yes, linen wrinkles. This is not a flaw — it's part of what makes linen linen. The wrinkle pattern is natural and becomes part of the fabric's lived-in character.

That said, there are ways to minimise excessive creasing:

- Remove from the washing machine or dryer promptly
- Hang while slightly damp and let gravity do the work
- Iron while damp on a high setting with a steam iron
- Accept and embrace the natural texture — a lightly wrinkled linen shirt looks intentional and relaxed, not sloppy

## How to care for linen

1. **Machine wash** on a gentle cycle with cold or warm water (not hot)
2. **Line dry** rather than tumble drying where possible
3. **Iron while damp** — linen is much easier to iron slightly wet
4. **Don't wring** — shake out gently and hang to dry
5. **Store loosely** — don't pack tightly folded, which sets creases

## When to choose linen over cotton

Choose linen when:
- You want maximum breathability in warm weather
- You want a fabric that improves with age
- You value natural, tactile texture
- You're making kurtas, summer shirts, or occasion wear
- You want something with genuine character and heritage

Choose cotton when:
- You need a fabric that wrinkles minimally
- You're making something that needs precise, crisp finishing
- The budget is tighter (linen is premium-priced)
